# Site audit, 5 October 2026

Everything a visitor, a search engine or a screen reader meets on
ukpropertyinsight.co.uk, checked on the live site on 5 October 2026.
Every fault found was fixed the same day. This records what was found,
what was fixed, and what to do next, in order. The search side is in
more depth in [seo-2026-10-05.md](seo-2026-10-05.md).

## The short answer

- **The site is technically sound.** Pages load in about a second on a
  slow phone connection, no internal link is broken, every data source
  answers, and after today the ten page types people land on have no
  accessibility faults.
- **Seven faults on the site were found and fixed, and three in the
  tools that check it.** Three of the seven cost search traffic or sales
  directly: a second copy of the whole site that Google could index,
  comparison pages that dropped out of Google after one slow lookup, and
  a Premium page whose Premium column was off the screen on a phone.
- **What holds the business back is not technical.** People find the
  site and use it: 117 reports were built for visitors this week. Very
  few come back for a second property, and the second property is
  where people subscribe.

## Faults found and fixed today

1. **The whole site answered on a second address.** The Render address
   (propertyvalue-eg3v.onrender.com) served every page with a canonical
   naming itself, a full second copy for Google to choose between, and
   the page cache then carried that canonical onto the real domain. The
   Render address now forwards to the real one; Stripe's payment
   notifications and health checks are left alone. (74cff08)
2. **Comparison pages dropped out of Google after one slow lookup.** A
   comparison whose sales lookup timed out was kept for the life of the
   cache without its price, and a page without a price tells Google not
   to index it. BH1 against BH4 was listed in the sitemap and hidden at
   the same time. A timed-out answer is no longer kept, and a missing
   price is taken from the area guide's own figure. (74cff08)
3. **A parent with good news was pointed the wrong way.** When a
   postcode came back inside a school's distance, the only next step
   was a link in the small print to check a different house. It is
   now a clear button, "Run the full report on" that postcode, tagged
   so it can be counted. (262fa42)
4. **Titles were cut off in Google.** 27 of 84 sampled titles ran past
   the 60 or so characters Google shows, and the part cut off was the
   answer: the distance, the band. Comparisons drop the brand, council
   tax pages drop "Bands A to H" only when a long council name needs the
   room, long school names use "CofE" and drop the year, and admissions
   hubs now say "catchment areas", the phrase people search for.
   A fresh sample afterwards found 3 of 84: two schools whose official
   names alone are that long, and the entrance tests page, shortened
   too. (b79b88f)
5. **Screen reader and keyboard faults.** Search boxes announced a list
   without saying they were search boxes with suggestions, the badge
   code box on every school page had no label, Google drew 91 nameless
   buttons on one schools guide page, maps told assistive technology they
   were pictures, and tables wider than a phone could not be reached by
   keyboard. Nothing changes to look at. (e6097a3, 093ac06)
6. **On a phone, the Premium page hid Premium.** Its comparison table
   showed the row names and nothing else: the Free account column was
   cut off and the Premium column was off the screen entirely, with no
   sign it was there. It now stacks on a phone, as the homepage's table
   does. Most search visitors are on phones. (093ac06)
7. **The revenue figure on /admin counted a test account.** Every other
   account figure on that page leaves test accounts out; the monthly
   revenue did not, and read £38.30 from four subscribers. Customers pay
   £28.31 from three. It now leaves them out and says so.

And in the tools:

- **seo_check.py** had read the sitemap index as if it were pages since
  the sitemap was split on 26 September, so it checked almost nothing.
  It now follows each child sitemap, measures titles as Google displays
  them, can list every problem (`--all`), and no longer logs 84
  Googlebot visits on /admin each time it runs.
- **audit_site.py** checked labels on input boxes only. It now checks
  text boxes and dropdowns too, which would have caught the badge box.
- **check_a11y.py** is new: the browser accessibility check behind
  fault 5, set in CLAUDE.md to run monthly with check_freshness.py.

## Healthy, no action needed

- **Speed.** Server response 0.17 to 0.36 s and the largest content
  drawn in 0.8 to 1.1 s on a throttled phone. School pages are the
  heaviest at 388 KB, most of it the Google map; other page types are
  about 100 KB.
- **Links, sources and copy.** Nothing broken, nothing stale.
- **Security headers.** In place. There is no Content Security Policy,
  deliberately: the stylesheet and several scripts are inlined for
  speed, so one would mean reworking every template before it
  protected anything, as main.py explains.

## What to improve next, in order

**1. Bring people back for the second property.** Conversion happens on
a return visit, almost never on the first, and the paywall, which only
an account coming back for another property can reach, was met 7 times
in two weeks by at most three accounts. The hook that should bring
parents back already exists: save a school and be told when the
council republishes its distance. It is barely used.

- *Ready to build when you say yes:* when someone follows "Sign up to
  save this school" from a school page, save the school as part of
  signing up, instead of sending them back to press Save a second time.
- *Your decision:* let a visitor without an account ask for a single
  email when that school's new distance is published. Every email the
  site sends today goes to an account holder, so this needs a decision
  on the email promise ("never on a schedule": this would be one email,
  sent when the figure appears) and on verification, which is dark until
  a Resend sending domain is verified.

**2. Measure where Google visitors turn into reports, then fix the
weakest page type.** The school-page button only became countable
today, so next week's figure is the first honest one. Two weeks of the
tagged sources will show which landing page hands over worst; that one
gets the same treatment the school verdict got.

**3. Links from other sites.** This is the ceiling between position 8
and the top three, set out in seo-2026-10-05.md. The pages others can
cite already exist: the entrance tests page with all 163 grammar
schools, appeals by council, council tax by council.

**4. Make each page in a family more its own.** Pages in the same
family share a lot of identical phrasing. Across three samples:
admissions hubs 49 to 66%, council tax 49 to 60%, school pages 55 to
59%, comparisons about 50%, area guides 41 to 43%. Only four pages of
a small family are sampled each run, so the figure moves between
runs; the order does not. Google reads near-identical pages as one, and
the 209 "crawled, not indexed" pages are usually that. Two fixes, both
from data already held: say each page's own figures in more of its
sentences (a council's Band D over six years and against its
neighbours; a hub's appeals, absence and entrance test), and shorten
the explanation every page repeats to one line linking to the
admissions guide. Admissions hubs first: 88 pages, and the best click
rate on the site.

**5. A "people" figure on /admin you can trust.** Crawlers that look
like browsers still count as people. Counting a visit only when the
page's own script reports that it ran would fix that without a cookie
or a third party, within the privacy policy. *Your decision*, because
it changes what the dashboard has counted until now.

**6. Load the school page map only when it scrolls into view.** It is
about 280 KB on every school page view and each one counts towards the
Google Maps allowance. Speed is already good, so this is about cost.
*Ready to build when you say yes.*

**7. Smaller.** 19 to 21 of 84 sampled descriptions run past what Google
shows; Google rewrites most descriptions anyway, and the council tax
ones are long on purpose (16 September). The /alternatives table still
scrolls sideways on a phone; it is reachable by keyboard now, and
stacking five long columns would make a very long page.
