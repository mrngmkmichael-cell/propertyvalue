# Site audit, 1 October 2026

What was checked, what was wrong, what was fixed, and what the site
could tell people next. Everything below was run against the live site
or against the publishers' own endpoints, not against a local copy.

## Errors found and fixed

**1. Ofsted inspection outcomes were two months behind.**
`scripts/import_schools.py` was pinned to the 30 June 2026 management
information file. Ofsted had published 31 August. Repointed and
re-imported: 21,957 rows read, 21,901 schools matched
(graded 7,834, ungraded 8,083, report card 2,441, none 3,543).
Live now, because dev and production read the same database.

**2. Private rents were three editions behind.**
`scripts/import_rental_prices.py` was pinned to the 22 July 2026
edition of the ONS Price Index of Private Rents, whose newest month
was June. Rather than move the pin, the importer now follows the ONS
`/current/` link, falling back to the newest dated `.xlsx` on the
dataset page. Re-imported: August 2026 for all 316 local authorities.
Manchester reads £1,373 a month overall, £1,233 for a two bed.

**3. Nothing we run would have caught either one.**
This is the real finding. `check_sources.py` asks whether a source
answers. `smoke.py` asks whether pages render. `audit_site.py` reads
the copy. All three were green the whole time, because a pinned URL
does not fail, it quietly serves last spring.

`scripts/check_freshness.py` now asks the missing question: is what we
hold still the newest the publisher has? Nine datasets. Six are exact,
the publisher states its newest vintage and we read it (Ofsted files,
KS4, KS2, leaver destinations, the school census, DEFRA air quality).
Three have no cheap statement of currency, so they get a lag budget
and the output says "not stated" rather than implying a fact it has
not checked. Seven tests pin the two failures above so the check
cannot lose its teeth. CLAUDE.md now says to run it on the first
session of each month.

Today it reads: nothing is behind its publisher.

## Checked and found correct

- `pytest`: 858 passed (851 before the new tests).
- `smoke.py` against production: 57 of 57.
- `audit_site.py` against production: clean, 52 pages, 44 internal links.
- `check_sources.py`: 39 sources across 6 addresses, none failed
  everywhere it was tried. The `amenities` and `orientation` failures
  are Overpass rate limiting under a burst, they pass when spaced.
- Console and network sweep of 23 live pages: 21 clean. The two flagged
  pages are a headless-browser artefact on the Google map, confirmed by
  eye in a real browser.
- **Leaver destinations stop at 2022/23 and air quality at 2024 because
  that is as far as the publishers have got.** The DfE destinations
  data set, version 1.0.1 published 24 July 2026, ends at 2022/23.
  DEFRA's 2025 maps return 404. Neither is our drift.
- School fullness (places against pupils on roll) is already built and
  shown, from Get Information About Schools.

## What the site could tell people next

Ranked by what is verified to exist, what it answers for a buyer, and
what it costs to build.

**1. Road collisions near this address. Verified.**
DfT STATS19, `dft-road-casualty-statistics-collision-2024.csv`, fetched
today. Every collision carries a latitude and longitude, severity,
number of casualties, number of vehicles, date and time. A five year
file is published alongside. Great Britain: England, Scotland and
Wales. Northern Ireland publishes separately through the PSNI, so an
NI postcode would say not covered, as the other England-only checks do.

Why it belongs: the report already answers what the building is like
and what the area is like. A junction that has put people in hospital
four times in five years is exactly the thing a viewing does not show
you, and nobody selling the house will mention it.

Effort: one importer, a spatial query by radius, one card. Medium.

**2. How this council handles planning applications. Verified.**
MHCLG live tables on planning application statistics, tables P120 to
P138, reference tables dated June 2026, fetched today. Per district
planning authority: how many applications were decided, how many were
granted, and how quickly.

Why it belongs: "could I extend it" is one of the first questions
about a house that needs work, and the honest answer is partly about
the council, not the house. This is per council, so it sits on the
council hubs and in the council section of the report.

Effort: one importer against an ODS file, a council lookup we already
have. Low to medium.

**3. School absence by council. Half verified.**
The DfE publishes absence to 2024/25, but the API exposes only local
authority, regional and national levels. School level exists in the
performance tables download, which I have not yet confirmed. The
council level figure is usable today; the school level figure, which
is the one people actually want, needs a day of work to confirm.

**4. NHS dentists accepting new patients. Not recommended.**
There is no open national dataset. The NHS service search needs an API
key, and without one this would mean scraping a page that is built in
JavaScript. By the standing rule, no reliable source means no feature.
Worth revisiting only if we get the key.

## Not covered by this audit

Page speed and Core Web Vitals, the paid report's PDF, the Chrome
extension, and anything behind sign-in. Those need their own pass.

## Built the same day

All three of the ranked items above were built, verified and shipped on
1 October.

**Road Safety**, the forty-fifth check and free. Collisions within 500
metres by severity, the most serious five named with distance and
month, from the DfT's STATS19 returns for 2021 to 2025. Half a million
collisions packed into a 7 MB local file rather than a database table,
because the database was at 499 MB of 512 MB. Northern Ireland is told
the check does not reach it.

**Planning Decisions**, the forty-sixth check and Premium. What the
council decided over the year to June 2026, what share it granted, how
often it agreed an extension of time, and how much it leaves to
officers, each against England. It gives no verdict, and the card says
why a grant rate is not a score.

**School absence**, on every council admissions hub. Days missed and
children missing them, primary and secondary, against England, for
2024/25. A council figure and never a school one, in the same words the
appeals block uses.

One thing the audit found on the way: the page cache was writing rows
into the database and never removing any, 22,050 of them and 48 MB
inside a database already at 499 MB. It now sweeps on write. That is
the same shape as the outage in August.
