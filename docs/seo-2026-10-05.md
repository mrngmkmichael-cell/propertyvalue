three months to 2 October, page indexing to 21 September) and checked
against the live pages.

The last fortnight: 65 clicks one week, 69 the next, while impressions

**School pages and the admissions hubs are the engine: 151 of the 238

2026/27" answer the search in the result itself and still get the click,
because the searcher wants the map and the history behind the number.

Most of the searches that bring those clicks do not appear in the
queries list at all. Google hides rare searches for privacy, and "west
hill school catchment area" is exactly that kind of search. The pages
table is the true picture; the queries table understates it.

## The honest answer to "the first few results"

**For the searches this site wins, yes, and it is already close.**
School catchment searches by school name, and council tax searches by
council, put the site on page one at positions 4 to 10. Moving those to
the top three is realistic over the coming months.

**For broad searches, no.** "Council tax", "house prices", "private
schools Birmingham" and "mortgage calculator" belong to gov.uk,
Rightmove, Zoopla, the Good Schools Guide and the banks, sites with
twenty years of links. The site briefly reached position 9 for plain
"council tax" (1,093 impressions, no clicks) because people searching
that want to pay their bill, not read about it. Chasing those is
not where the time should go.

No search with twenty or more impressions currently sits in the top
three. That is the gap to close, and it is a gap of authority more than
of content.

## What moves a page from position 8 to position 3, in order

**1. Links from other sites. This is the ceiling.** The domain is six
weeks old in Google's eyes and almost nothing links to it. Every site
above it on a school search has years of links. Content alone gets a
page to position 8; links get it to 3. The legitimate sources for a site
like this are people who already use it: the buyer's agents and
solicitors in the outreach routine (ask the ones who reply to link to
the free checker from their own site), parents' forums and local groups
where catchments are argued about (a link someone shares because the
map answered their question), and the data pages that give a journalist
or a blogger a number to cite, such as the appeals league table, the
entrance tests page and council tax by council. Nothing bought, nothing
swapped, nothing posted under a false name: Google punishes all three.

**2. Time and freshness.** New pages climb for months without anything
changing. The October data refresh (rents, Ofsted, absence, planning,
collisions) matters here: Google prefers a page that says 2026/27 and
means it.

**3. Internal links to the engine pages.** School pages are what
people click, so every page that can point at the right school should.
The newest candidate: the entrance tests page names 35 selective areas
but does not yet list the 163 grammar schools in them, each of which
has a school page people search for by name.

**4. Stop wasting Google's crawl.** As of 21 September, 7,027 pages were
indexed and 5,756 were not:

| Why not indexed | Pages | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Discovered, not yet crawled | 3,104 | Google knows them and has not got round to them. The sitemap split on 26 Sep should help; this data predates it |
| Not found (404) | 1,509 | Links pointing at pages that do not exist. Needs the list to find where they come from |
| Page with redirect | 822 | Normal, old addresses forwarding to new ones |
| Crawled, not indexed | 209 | Google read them and passed. Usually thin or near-duplicate pages |
| Excluded by noindex | 66 | Deliberate, such as login pages |
| Server error (5xx) | 9 | Should be none. Needs the list |

The 404s and the server errors need the example URLs, which the summary
export does not include.

## The council tax pages: why page one and no clicks

186 pages, nearly 11,000 impressions at an average position of 9.7, and
a click rate of 0.2%. The reason is visible in the result: the
description lists every band's bill, so someone searching "Ashford
council tax band C" reads the answer in Google and never needs to come
in. That was a deliberate change on 16 September, made because when the
result showed only Band D the council's own site took the click instead.

Both versions lose the click on a search the result can answer outright,
which is why I have not flipped it back. Those impressions are not
wasted: they are Google deciding the pages are the right answer for
thousands of searches, which is exactly the authority signal the rest of
the site needs.

## Area guides and private school listings: page four

Both families sit around position 37. Two specific openings:

- **Area guides are being found for "is M20 a good place to live" and
  "M20 area reviews"** (positions 9 to 15). The guides answer that with
  data but never say it in those words. A short section framed around
  the question, answered with the official figures and no verdict, would
  meet the search where it is. The evidence is still one district, so it
  is worth watching before rebuilding every area guide around it.
- **Private school listings** compete with sites that publish fees,
  which the Department for Education register does not hold. Without a
  real source for fees, these will stay where they are, and the rule is
  not to invent one.
