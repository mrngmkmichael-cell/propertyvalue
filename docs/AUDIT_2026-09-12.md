# Site audit and competitor report, 12 September 2026

Written after Michael asked two things: run a full audit, and say why
defects he found today felt like they should have been fixed long ago.
The second question is the more useful one, so it goes first.

---

## 1. Why today's three defects survived

Three things were found today by reading live pages, not by any tool:

- the homepage hero said "40 checks" while the same page said 44, three
  times, because the figure was a literal in `main.py` outside the reach
  of `scripts/bump_check_count.py`;
- `/premium` explained the difference between the pass and the
  subscription, in the visible FAQ and in the FAQ structured data Google
  reads, while the pass has had no button since 5 September;
- `/running-costs`, the busiest page on the site, told a mistyped
  postcode to use a search box that had been removed from that page the
  day before.

**The first one was catchable and nobody ran the tool.**
`scripts/audit_site.py` has a rule for exactly this: "every check count
must match the hero's own figure". Run against production today it
reports **clean, 51 pages, 150 links, 0 issue groups**, and it reports
clean because the defect was fixed this morning. It would have caught it
the day the number drifted.

The reason it never ran is in `CLAUDE.md`, which names the pre-push
routine as pytest and then `smoke.py`. `audit_site.py` is not in that
sentence, so it is a tool someone has to remember. Nobody did, for
however many days "40" was wrong.

**The other two were not catchable by anything we have.** Both are the
same shape, and it is a shape neither tests nor smoke can see:

> The page contradicts something outside the page.

A test asserts a page renders. Smoke asserts it answers 200. Neither
knows that a box was deleted yesterday, or that a Stripe price ID is
unset. The copy was true when written and became false when something
else changed.

### The size of that class

A scan of every number stated as fact in the templates, excluding
structural values and Jinja-derived ones, finds **74 hardcoded claims**.
The live ones were checked against the data today and are all currently
true:

| Claim | Where | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| "more than 3,600 English schools across 88 councils" | homepage FAQ, twice, one in FAQ structured data | 3,627 schools, 88 councils |
| "2,943 districts" | homepage caption | 2,943 |
| "44 checks" | many pages | CHECK_COUNT is 44 |
| "350 councils" | council tax pages | matches |

All true, none pinned. Every one of them is the 40-checks defect waiting
to happen: a fact typed into a template that cannot follow the thing it
describes. The school figure is the worst of them, because it is baked
into structured data Google may show as a rich result.

### What to do about it

1. **Put `audit_site.py` in the pre-push routine**, in CLAUDE.md, beside
   pytest and smoke. It already earns its place and costs one command.
2. **Add three rules to it**, each aimed at a real failure that happened:
   - copy that names a UI element must find that element on the same
     page (catches "the search at the top of the page");
   - copy that names a plan must find a way to buy that plan (catches
     the pass FAQ);
   - the counts above are read from the database and compared, so a
     school import that moves 88 to 89 fails the audit rather than
     quietly making the homepage wrong.
3. **Derive rather than type.** `CHECK_COUNT` went in today. The school
   and council figures should follow: they are already in the database
   the page could read.

## 2. The rest of the audit

**Tooling, run today against production.** `audit_site.py`: clean, 51
pages, 150 links. `smoke.py`: 54 of 54 passed. Page timings are good:
the homepage, `/premium`, `/browser-extension` and `/alternatives` all
answer in about a quarter of a second, area guides and council pages
under a second.

**The one measured performance defect is the shared cache.** The M1 1AE
sample report, which the homepage advertises as its one zero-typing
route in, answered in 3.66s at 11:05 and had gone fully cold by 11:20,
hitting the 12-second crawler deadline. The finished-HTML cache and the
report-gather cache share one store of 48 MB and 1,500 entries, and a
single `/schools/guide?q=` page is 290 to 450 KB, so roughly 110 to 165
cached pages evict everything else. A crawler took 603 of those pages in
seven hours last night. Partitioning that store is the fix and it is not
the warming cadence rejected on 7 September.

**Conversion is the real problem and it is not the paywall.** 41 of 56
real accounts used the site on exactly one day and not one of them has
ever subscribed. 15 came back on another day and 2 of those subscribed.
16 accounts have ever reached the paywall and 2 bought, which is a normal
rate; only 5 reached it in the six days to 12 September, which is not.
Full working in `project_conversion_is_a_return`.

## 3. Competitor report

Read from their own live pages on 12 September 2026. The 9 September
figures are in `reference_competitors`; Propbar's pricing has changed
since then and `/alternatives` needs updating with it.

### Propbar

| | |
|---|---|
| Monthly | £49.99 |
| 3 months | £34.99/mo, billed £104.97 |
| 6 months | £24.99/mo, billed £149.94, marked most popular |
| Scope | 47 risk checks, 14 official data sources, England and Wales |

The pricing page is built around one idea: **"Most buyers complete their
search in 4 to 6 months. Subscribe for the search. Cancel when it ends."**
Everything supports it. A £0.83/day anchor. A timeline graphic of a
six-month search. A comparison of 28 properties checked by the average
buyer against £200 for one paid report elsewhere. Testimonials chosen
because they say the customer cancelled: "I have bought a home so don't
need it any more. Loved the service."

They also publish what they do not cover, on the pricing page, as a
trust signal: Scotland partial, Northern Ireland unsupported, new builds
under 30 days not recorded. The 6-month plan includes a free partner
account so two people can search together.

### Locrating

| | |
|---|---|
| Basic | Free |
| Monthly | £12.99 recurring |
| Single month | £14.99, does not renew |
| 3 months | £41.99, does not renew |

Established 2010, "trusted by millions of parents a year", "often
copied, never bettered". Shows 6,092 member ratings live with the full
star distribution, 4.5 out of 5, updated from members' own dashboards.
Reviews are dated and specific.

Two things to note. One customer review is doing sales work we cannot
match: *"I purchased the 3 month membership, which came with a ton of
useful features and doesn't auto-renew, which is perfect for the length
of a house hunt."* And their pricing page attacks us by category:
**"Don't be fooled by free sites. Free and AI generated sites are a
false economy; they are often inaccurate."**

### Crystal Roof

Free, area level rather than address level, ten categories of postcode
statistics. Owns StreetCheck. Now leads with **"New: the most
comprehensive school guide"**, which is a move into the area we are
strongest in, and it carries area reviews from users.

## 4. Where each side is better

### They are better than us at

1. **The shape of the price.** Propbar and Locrating both sell the
   length of a house hunt, and both make not-renewing a feature. We sell
   £9.99 monthly and £24.99 quarterly, both auto-renewing, and the one
   product that matched a house hunt, the £19 pass, has been off sale
   since 5 September. This is the clearest gap on the list and it points
   straight at our conversion problem: our accounts do not come back,
   and we are asking them for an open-ended commitment at the exact
   moment they are thinking about one house.
2. **Social proof.** Locrating shows 6,092 live ratings. We show
   nothing. Our reviews table has zero rows and Trustpilot's rules stop
   us rendering scores. This is the one place a six-week-old domain
   simply loses.
3. **Coverage honesty as marketing.** Propbar puts what it cannot do on
   the pricing page and makes it a reason to trust the rest. We do this
   throughout the product and never once as a selling point.
4. **A second person on the account.** Propbar's six-month plan includes
   a partner account. Homes are usually bought by two people. We have no
   concept of a second person, and a second person is also a second
   reason to come back.
5. **School depth.** Locrating has feeder schools, destination schools,
   priority areas and pupils' home locations. We have published
   admission distances, which is a different and in some ways better
   thing, but their coverage of the school question is wider.

### We are better than them at

1. **Price, by a distance.** £9.99 a month against Propbar's £49.99. We
   are a fifth of the price of the closest full competitor and we barely
   say so.
2. **The admission answer.** We measure a specific address against the
   council's own published last-distance-offered for 3,627 schools
   across 88 councils and say Likely, Borderline or Unlikely. Locrating
   shows the distance; we answer the question the distance raises.
3. **Sourcing discipline.** Every figure names the body that published
   it, nothing is modelled without saying so, and gaps are stated in
   words. That is the exact answer to Locrating's "free and AI generated
   sites are often inaccurate", and it is currently an internal rule
   rather than a public argument.
4. **The free tier.** 26 checks on any address with no account and no
   card. Crystal Roof is free but area level; Propbar's free tier is
   thinner than ours.
5. **Running costs as a first-class feature.** Council tax by band with
   the discounts nobody mentions, the EPC's own energy estimate, tenure,
   stamp duty. Neither of the other two treats the bills as a feature.
6. **The 25-page PDF.** Nothing comparable was visible on either rival.

## 5. What I would do, in order

1. **Put a house-hunt-shaped product back on sale.** Both rivals have
   one and both make non-renewal the selling point. Ours is built, it
   leads the pricing page when enabled, and it is one Stripe price ID
   away. Michael's call, held since 5 September, but the competitor
   evidence is new since then and it lands on our worst metric.
2. **Partition the cache.** A 13-second sample report on the homepage's
   own link is the first impression for anyone arriving cold.
3. **Add the three audit rules and put the audit in the pre-push
   routine.** This is the answer to "why wasn't this fixed long ago".
4. **Say the price difference out loud.** A fifth of Propbar's price,
   with the same sources, is the strongest sentence we are not using.
5. **Answer the "AI generated sites are inaccurate" attack head on.**
   We have the best possible answer and it is buried in `/methodology`.
6. **Derive the school and council figures** from the database so the
   homepage cannot go stale, starting with the ones inside structured
   data.
7. **Update `/alternatives`** with Propbar's new pricing and move
   `ALTERNATIVES_CHECKED_ON`. A comparison page naming rivals is worse
   than none when it is stale, and it is three days out of date today.
