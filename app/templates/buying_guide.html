How a UK property purchase works

  1. Get an Agreement in Principle first. Agents won't take an offer seriously without one. Budget for Stamp Duty, solicitor, survey, lender fees and removals on top of the price.
  2. Make an offer. Not binding until exchange (step 5) in England, Wales and NI, so either side can walk away until then. Scotland binds much earlier, once missives conclude.
  3. Instruct a solicitor. Book a survey. The lender's valuation is not a survey and won't flag defects. Which survey level is below.
  4. Apply for the formal mortgage offer. Only possible once there's a specific property and an agreed price.
  5. Searches, then exchange. Local authority, drainage and environmental searches (a report on this site covers much of this for free, first). Exchange is the point of no return: deposit paid, completion date fixed.
  6. Completion. Balance paid, keys handed over. Days to weeks after exchange, by agreement.
  7. After completion. Your solicitor files the Stamp Duty return and registers you with Land Registry. Check it's in their fee.

Tax on buying and owning property

Stamp Duty (or equivalent)

  • One-off, paid on completion. SDLT in England and NI, LTT in Wales, LBTT in Scotland.
  • Banded, not flat. Each rate applies only to the slice of the price inside its band. First-time buyers and additional properties have their own bands.
  • Bands change at budgets. The calculator on any property report here is kept current and does the maths.

Capital Gains Tax

  • Only when you sell, only on the gain. Second homes and buy-to-lets. Your main home is normally exempt.
  • Rates and allowance move. Current figures: gov.uk.

Council Tax

  • Annual, set by band. A to H in England and Scotland, Wales has its own. Based on a 1991 valuation (2003 in Wales), not today's price.
  • Look up any address free at gov.uk/council-tax-bands. It isn't open data, so reports here don't show it.

Things to check before you commit

  • Lease under 80 years is a problem. Expensive to extend, hard to mortgage. Check years left, ground rent and service charge before offering.
  • Pick the survey level. Level 1 for newer and sound. Level 2 is the usual choice. Level 3 for older, larger or altered homes.
  • Run the free report first. Flood, subsidence, radon, contamination, mining, planning, sold prices, EPC, crime and schools, before you pay anyone.
  • Chain-free is safer. New build, probate, or a seller who has already moved. Chains are why purchases collapse.
  • Book solicitor and surveyor before the offer. Prices vary a lot. Being ready shortens everything after.
