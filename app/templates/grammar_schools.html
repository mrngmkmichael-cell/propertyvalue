Grammar schools in England: all {{ schools | length }}, by council

The state-funded secondary schools whose admissions policy on the Department for Education's register is selective. A grammar school admits by its entrance test first and by its oversubscription criteria second, so where a council publishes how far the last place went, that distance applies after the pass mark. The tests come from a handful of bodies, and their own free familiarisation papers are linked below; we set no tests of our own.

How selection works, and what the address decides

In most areas a child sits the test in September of Year 6, after registering in the summer term before; results arrive in October and the secondary application closes on 31 October. A pass makes a child eligible, not admitted: schools with more eligible applicants than places rank them by their published criteria, which range from test score alone to priority areas and distance. Where a school here shows a last distance offered, that is the figure the council published for the most recent round, and it tells you whether a house closer to the school mattered. Registration windows and test dates are set each year by the council or the school, so check the school's own admissions page before relying on a date.
