Was this home in the news?

{% set news = news_search(transactions, location) %}

There is no official record of deaths, crimes or incidents at individual addresses in the UK. Coroners, the police and the Office for National Statistics publish no address-level register, and a seller does not have to volunteer a death or a crime in the property. Rather than guess, this card says what is missing and how to check.

What the report can and cannot show

The Crime & Safety card counts offences within about a mile, from Police.uk. Police.uk moves every incident to a point on or near a street segment on purpose, so that no home can be identified. It can show what the street sees; it cannot say whether anything happened inside this property. Nothing official records a haunting, so the report says nothing about those.

Check the news yourself

Local papers report incidents by street and rarely by house number, so search the street and read each story for the number and the year before drawing a conclusion.

The step that protects you

Ask the seller's solicitor, in writing, in the pre-contract enquiries: "Has anyone died in the property, and has it been the scene of any crime or police investigation?" A false written answer is a misrepresentation you can act on; silence is not. In England and Wales, Sykes v Taylor-Rose (Court of Appeal, 2004) held that a seller who answered the standard form honestly had no duty to volunteer that a murder had taken place in the house. Put the same question to the estate agent in writing: agents must not withhold information that would change an average buyer's decision, a duty carried into the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024.
