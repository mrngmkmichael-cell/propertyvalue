Primary places, and why infant appeals are different

A reception, year 1 or year 2 appeal is heard under a separate rule. Where admitting another child would push an infant class past 30, the panel may only allow the appeal on narrow legal grounds: that the arrangements were applied wrongly or unlawfully, or that the decision was one no reasonable panel would have made. What these figures are, and what they are not

They belong to councils, not to schools. The Department for Education collects appeals against community and voluntary controlled schools as council totals, and does not publish the school-level returns academies and voluntary aided schools make. Appeals are not published school by school, so nobody can tell you how often appeals against one named school succeed, and this page does not pretend to.

They describe a round that has finished. The {{ summary.year }} release covers appeals decided by 1 September {{ summary.year | int - 1 }}, against places for that September. A panel decides your case on its own facts.

Lodged, heard and allowed are three different numbers. Some appeals are withdrawn when a place comes up, and some are settled before a hearing, which is why fewer are heard than lodged. The share allowed is always of the appeals actually heard.

A high rate is not a promise. It usually means places were still available somewhere in that council. A low one usually means the schools appealed against were genuinely full.
